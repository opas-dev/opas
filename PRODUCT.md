# Product

## Register

product

## Users

OPAS serves technical leaders, documentation teams, support-content authors, and self-hosters who need to publish and operate a help center without handing control of their content, branding, or infrastructure to a per-seat SaaS vendor. Authors work in a focused admin interface; readers arrive with a question and need a trustworthy answer quickly; automated agents need the same content in predictable machine-readable forms.

## Product Purpose

OPAS is an open-source, runtime-themable, deploy-anywhere help center. It succeeds when a team can author and publish structured support content, restyle the entire public experience without rebuilding, deploy the same product to a container, Vercel, or Cloudflare Workers, and expose useful browser and agent-readable documentation from one source of truth.

## Brand Personality

Capable, transparent, independent. OPAS should feel calm under operational pressure, direct about what it does, and respectful of the user's ownership. The interface earns confidence through clarity and reliability rather than spectacle.

## Anti-references

- Expensive helpdesk-suite clutter that makes a knowledge base feel subordinate to ticketing.
- Generic AI SaaS marketing: purple gradients, glass panels, glowing abstractions, and inflated claims.
- Endless identical card grids, decorative dashboards, and novelty controls that slow down routine work.
- Proprietary documentation products that obscure content ownership or make customization feel gated.

## Design Principles

- Get the reader to the answer: search, taxonomy, and article typography serve fast comprehension.
- Make ownership visible: content, themes, deployment, and machine-readable surfaces remain inspectable and portable.
- Practice operational calm: familiar controls, honest states, and restrained motion keep authors in flow.
- Let themes carry identity: the shell remains coherent while runtime tokens create materially different brands.
- Keep the public and admin experiences equally credible: publishing tools should feel as deliberate as the help center they produce.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Meet WCAG 2.2 AA across public and admin surfaces. Preserve semantic heading order, complete keyboard operation, visible focus, at least 4.5:1 body-text contrast, non-color state cues, readable line lengths, touch targets appropriate for mobile, and reduced-motion behavior for every transition.
